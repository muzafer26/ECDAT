THE CHALLENGE

Complexity Slows Migration

When cryptographic usage is difficult to discover and understand, migration planning slows. Repositories, dependencies, CI/CD pipelines, containers, certificates, configurations, and network endpoints can contain different pieces of the cryptographic picture, creating blind spots, unsupported assumptions, and uncertainty about quantum exposure.

01

Discovery Fragmentation

Cryptography is spread across application code, certificates, libraries, protocols, infrastructure, and third-party systems. Different tools see different parts of it, leaving teams to piece the inventory together.

02

Context Gaps

Finding “RSA” or “ECDSA” is not enough. Teams still need to know where it is used, what it protects, which systems depend on it, and how difficult it will be to change.

03

Decision Uncertainty

A discovery result may be missing key size, protocol role, ownership, or operational context. ECDAT keeps those gaps visible instead of turning incomplete evidence into a confident conclusion.

Traditional Discovery Output

  • Raw scanner alerts: "RSA found in file.java"
  • Opaque severity scores without auditable rationale
  • Guesses or assumes missing properties like bit-length
  • No awareness of operational deployment context
  • Leaves migration planning and target selection to guesswork

The ECDAT Approach

  • What evidence supports this? Auditable line and source code context
  • What is it being used for? Role-aware primitive classification
  • What does it mean? Formal classical status and quantum exposure
  • How uncertain is the result? Explicitly flags missing evidence
  • What migration options exist? NIST-standardized PQC candidates
  • What needs human review? Mandatory review gates before sign-off
  • What should happen next? Dependency-ordered milestone roadmap

What happens when ECDAT finds AES?

ECDAT does not treat every cryptographic finding as a public-key PQC migration candidate.

DISCOVERY AES usage detected.
ASSESSMENT Symmetric cryptography has a different quantum exposure model from RSA and elliptic-curve public-key cryptography.
MIGRATION No public-key PQC replacement is applicable.
DECISION Review key strength, usage, policy, and data sensitivity.
ROLE-AWARE ANALYSIS

What happens when ECDAT finds Ed25519?

DISCOVERY Ed25519 signature usage detected.
ASSESSMENT The public-key signature algorithm requires post-quantum migration assessment.
MIGRATION Candidate signature algorithms include ML-DSA and SLH-DSA.
DECISION Review signing dependencies, compatibility, trust relationships, and deployment impact.
PRODUCT PRINCIPLES

Built for Reviewable Decisions

01

Evidence Before Conclusions

ECDAT preserves the evidence behind a finding before deriving analytical conclusions from it.

02

Unknown Means Unknown

When available evidence does not establish a property, ECDAT represents the uncertainty instead of inventing an answer.

03

Context Matters

Algorithm names alone do not establish operational role, business impact, or migration requirements.

04

Migration Is Context-Dependent

PQC migration candidates depend on cryptographic role, protocol, dependencies, compatibility, and operational context.

05

Human Review Matters

Context-dependent migration decisions remain reviewable by the people responsible for security and engineering outcomes.
